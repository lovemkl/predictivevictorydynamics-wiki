## How the Sixteen Teams Are Built

The field divides into four recognizable roster archetypes, and every major result so far traces back to how these archetypes interact:

**The tempo teams.** Front-loaded burst compositions built to open fights with overwhelming damage inside the first rotation. Their matches either end quickly or go badly — a tempo roster that closes inside its opening window wins emphatically; one that meets sustained healing and shielding early tends to spend its resources and lose the long game.

**The attrition teams.** Healing-and-reaction compositions that win by refusing to lose. They convert fights into wars of sustainability, leaning on Energy Recharge and reaction uptime until the opponent's damage curve flattens. Across the resolved rounds, attrition teams own the event's best record against the public's expectations.

**The hybrid teams.** One premier carry plus supporting sustain — the most common late-bracket survivors. Hybrids can trade tempo for attrition depending on the matchup, which makes them the hardest to price and the most common source of line value.

**The gimmick teams.** Rosters built around a single interaction — a specific reaction chain, a shield-break engine, an AoE stacking comp. Gimmicks swing wildly: they either find their matchup and dominate it or meet a counter and collapse entirely. They are the source of most of the event's drama and most of its lost Emblems.

## Group-by-Group Results

**Group A** set the template in round one: the tempo favorite crashed out against an attrition roster that simply outlasted the opening burst. The community learned the event's core lesson on day one — damage-per-screenshot loses to damage-per-minute in auto-resolved simulations.

**Group B** broke the other way. The hybrid survivor handled its attrition opponent comfortably by pacing its own sustain instead of racing, proving the archetype matchup cuts both ways: hybrids lose tempo races to tempo teams and attrition races to attrition teams, but win the cross-matches where they choose the terms.

**Group C** produced the event's reference upset — the crowd's heavy favorite, a stats-laden tempo roster, ground down by superior Energy Recharge distribution and defensive scaling. Group C is why the daily predictions page weight-checks crowd sentiment on every board.

**Group D** restored order, with the field's most complete hybrid advancing without drama — the closest thing the bracket has shown to a default pick, and a team whose quarterfinal form carried into the late rounds.

## Quarterfinal Form Lines

With the Round of 16 resolved, the surviving eight produced form lines that matter for every remaining prediction:

- The **attrition survivors** have all needed to win at least one long fight, and all looked sustainable doing it. Their weakness is spike damage they cannot heal through — they beat tempo teams that fail to close, and lose only to complete rosters.
- The **hybrid survivors** have shown adaptive pacing, which is precisely the skill the semifinals reward. The bracket's remaining board is mostly hybrid-versus-attrition — the matchup profile that decided Groups B and C in opposite directions.
- No pure **gimmick team** survived the quarterfinals, confirming the archetype's ceiling: strong enough to upset one round, too fragile to survive a bracket that learns.

The through-line for your own predictions: at this stage of Predictive Victory Dynamics, roster quality is roughly equal among survivors, so the marginal edge has migrated from "who is stronger on paper" to "who can choose the terms of the fight." Favor teams whose win conditions are flexible, and discount teams that need their opponent to cooperate.

## Reading the Bracket for the Final Rounds

Semifinals and the grand final compress everything this guide has covered into single, high-stakes boards. Three rules hold:

**Form over fame.** By the final four, every team has a real resolve history. What a team did last round, against a comparable archetype, is better evidence than anything on its stat sheet.

**Tempo needs a fast clock; attrition needs no clock.** If a final-round matchup pits the two profiles, the question is never "who has better numbers" but "does the tempo side close before the sustain curve takes over" — and the quarterfinals suggest the event's simulation pacing currently favors the sustain curve.

**Respect line value.** Late-round odds overreact to narratives. A semifinal loser narrative — "they looked flat last round" — routinely inflates the price on their opponent beyond what the roster read justifies. The biggest single-match payouts of the entire event historically come from final boards where the crowd has written off a capable team on one underwhelming resolve.
